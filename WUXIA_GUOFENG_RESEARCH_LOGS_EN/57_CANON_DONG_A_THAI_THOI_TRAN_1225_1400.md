# CLASSICAL DAI VIET CANON: EARLY TRẦN DYNASTY (1225–1400)

> **Public Release:** 1.0 · **Scope:** Trần Dynasty, Đông A Worldbuilding, An Thái & Mộc Tĩnh Lan Narrative Arc  
> **Primary Historiographical Sources:** *Đại Việt Sử Ký Toàn Thư*, *Khâm Định Việt Sử Thông Giám Cương Mục*, *An Nam Chí Lược*, Archaeological records of Đông Sơn, Hoa Lư, and Trần sites.

## 0. Evidence Taxonomy & Labeling Rules

- **[VERIFIED HISTORICAL RECORD]:** Supported by verified historical chronicles, epigraphy, or archaeological remains.
- **[HISTORIOGRAPHICAL INTERPRETATION]:** Plausible contextual analysis, but not the sole historiographical consensus.
- **[MYTHOLOGICAL / FOLKLORE TRADITION]:** Myths, legends, folk songs, proverbs, and oral memories.
- **[CONTROLLED FICTIONAL NARRATIVE]:** Original creative characters, relationships, and events crafted for fiction without falsifying historical outcomes.

## 1. Historical Boundaries & Military Precision

1. **Strictly Disentangle the Three Mongol-Yuan Invasions:**
   - **1257–1258:** Led by Uriyangkhadai; battle of Bình Lệ Nguyên, tactical retreat to Lô River and Thiên Mạc, decisive counter-offensive victory at Đông Bộ Đầu (Dec 24).
   - **1284–1285:** Led by Toghon and Sogetu; presence of Omar.
   - **1287–1288:** Led by Toghon, Zhang Wenhu, and Omar; naval climax and stake ambush at the Bạch Đằng River.
2. The 1258 campaign **did not involve** massive three-deck warships, Omar, Sogetu, or Zhang Wenhu.
3. The Bạch Đằng stake field in 1288 was the specific context where logistics supply lines were severed by naval ambushes and timber stakes.
4. Troop strength for 1258 is recorded as approximately 20,000 to 30,000 troops, not an exaggerated round number.
5. Từ Hải and Thúy Kiều belong to the 16th century and must not be placed in the 13th-century Trần Dynasty.

| **1258** | Battle of Bình Lệ Nguyên; tactical river retreat; triumph at Đông Bộ Đầu | Infantry combat, river maneuver warfare; no giant ironclad fleets. |

## 3. Three Critical Narrative Scenes

### 3.1. Mount Yên Tử (1237)
**[VERIFIED HISTORICAL RECORD]** The king departed Thăng Long for Mount Yên Tử during royal disputes. **[HISTORIOGRAPHICAL INTERPRETATION]** The erection of the temporary hall affirmed that imperial authority was reconstituted wherever the royal court was situated.

### 3.2. Bình Lệ Nguyên (1258)
**[VERIFIED HISTORICAL RECORD]** King Trần Thái Tông conducted a strategic river retreat. Officer Nhật Kiệu wrote "Enter Song" on water; Chancellor Trần Thủ Độ declared: "My head has not yet fallen to the ground." Lê Phụ Trần used wooden boat boards to shield the king from volleys of enemy arrows.

### 3.3. Đông Bộ Đầu (December 24, 1258)
**[VERIFIED HISTORICAL RECORD]** Đại Việt forces launched a decisive counter-offensive, routing Mongol forces from the capital. Local tribal leader Hà Bổng ambushed the retreating forces at Quy Hóa.

## 4. Society, Dykes, Taxation & Granaries

- **[VERIFIED HISTORICAL RECORD]** The village (*xã*) was the fundamental unit of tax collection and communal mutual aid.
- **[VERIFIED HISTORICAL RECORD]** Public farmland was taxed at one-third of harvest yield; private farmland was taxed at one-tenth.
- **[VERIFIED HISTORICAL RECORD]** Construction of the Đỉnh Nhĩ dykes began in 1248, establishing the official government office of Dyke Commissioners (*Hà Đê Sứ*).
- **[CONTROLLED FICTIONAL NARRATIVE]** Sand-sieving methods, waterwheels, salt extraction tools, and hidden grain distribution are fictional narrative details, not formal imperial statutory regulations.

## 5. Regional Geography and Ecosystem

- **Northeastern Basin:** Lục Đầu River, Vạn Kiếp naval bases, tidal marshlands, bamboo forests.
- **Frontier Passes:** Chi Lăng, Bình Lệ Nguyên, Lô River, Cà Lồ River.
- **Midland Mountains:** Mount Yên Tử, Phù Vân peaks, sea of mist, mountain pine/bamboo forests, herbal storerooms.
- **Wet-Rice Plains:** Thiên Trường Palace, Đỉnh Nhĩ dykes, Red River delta.
- **Coastal Seaports:** Vân Đồn trade port, three-masted wooden junks, tidal navigation.
- **Artisan Villages:** Chu Đậu ceramics, Vạn Phúc silk weaving, Phong Khê bark paper (*Dó*).
- **Landscape Terminology:** Never use "steppes" or "arid deserts" for Vietnam. Use: *forested frontier ridges, alluvial riverbeds, dense reed marshlands, mountain bamboo valleys*.

## 7. Theological Boundaries & Belief Systems

1. **Heaven Reverence (*Tế Trời*):** Cultural worldview centered on gratitude to the Creator (*Ông Trời*), oaths of integrity, and natural balance.
2. **Trúc Lâm Zen School:** Historical 13th-century Zen tradition; free of commercialized superstitions or sorcery.
3. **Sacred Hymn Tradition:** Strictly independent biblical ecosystem; never cross-mixed into ethnic classical wuxia.
4. No five-clawed northern court dragons, magical levitation, demonic possession, or immortality alchemy as core powers.
5. **Giao Long:** Kept exclusively as the ancient Dong Son riverine water-totem.

## 8. Canon Characters

### An Thái — **[CONTROLLED FICTIONAL NARRATIVE]**
- Ancestral roots connected to Chu Đậu village and Lục Đầu River; grew from a young warrior into a river navigator, Bạch Đằng veteran, and master potter.
- Visual Identity: Indigo cross-collar tunic (*Áo Giao Lĩnh*), left cheek scar, Giao Long tattoo, carved wooden hairpin, "Sát Thát" pledge tattooed on wrist.
- Realistic limits: No magical flying, sword levitation, or futuristic anachronisms.

### Mộc Tĩnh Lan — **[CONTROLLED FICTIONAL NARRATIVE]**
- Master Chu Đậu ceramic artisan and keeper of Trúc Lâm herbal medicine.
- Visual Identity: Cross-collar or four-panel raw silk dress, clay-stained hands, lotus wooden hairpin, woven bamboo medicine satchel.
- Role: Treating battle wounds, preparing herbal remedies, tending pottery kilns; respectful partnership with An Thái based on mutual dignity.

## 9. Visual Aesthetic Canon

1. Concept art must reflect authentic geography, monsoon seasons, tides, and native materials.
2. Realistic weathering: characters exhibit authentic scars, worn leather, clay stains, salt spray, mud, and raw unbleached silk.
3. Military attires: laminated leather armor, ironwood, brass, fish skin, stakes, spears, and banners.
4. Traditional civilian attires: *Áo Giao Lĩnh, Áo Tứ Thân, Áo Viên Lĩnh*, conical palm hats, wooden clogs, indigo/brown vegetable dyes.
5. Strictly prohibited: northern five-clawed imperial dragons, Hanfu, kimono, qipao, glowing magical blades, futuristic cybernetics, or image watermarks.
