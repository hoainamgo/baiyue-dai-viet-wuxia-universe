- Ancient title: Giao Chi Que Vuong, Giao Giao Moc.
- Characteristics: Sweet and spicy taste, great heat, clears meridians, restores yang and saves troubles.
- Morphology & Context: Thick cinnamon bark, oozing fragrant oil like wild honey, grows on the slopes of Thuong Xuan limestone mountains (Thanh Hoa).
- Gypsy / Medical application: Use mixed with warm sticky rice wine and hold under the tongue when crossing cold snowy passes or being hit by cold swords and swords; Warms the dantian when practicing river and water internal exercises.

### 2. Agarwood & Ky Nam (Dai Ngan Truong Son)
- Ancient title: Moc Tinh Chi Soul, Hundred Years of Transformation Medicine.

### 3. Ngai Nhung & Ginger Wind (Thanh Ngai Dia Duoc)
- Ancient names: Kim Sang Than Thao, Khuong Hoang Da Thao.
- Properties: Bitter taste, warm properties, repels blood stasis, clears blood (quickly stops bleeding), eliminates wind and cold.
- Appearance & Context: Growing green around the edges of terraced fields and thatched porches; The upper surface of the leaves is dark green, the lower surface is covered with smooth silvery white hairs.
- Application: Crush and apply directly to cut wounds; A bundle of wormwood acupuncture points restores vitality after a long night of exercise.

### 4. Betel Rope & Areca Lien Phong (Great Natural Antibiotics)
- Ancient title: Nam Phuong Linh Diep.
- Properties: Spicy, warm, anti-inflammatory, antiseptic, warms the spleen, strengthens teeth, bones and joints.
- Appearance & Context: The climbing stem curves around the areca trunk, standing upright in the sky, with thick, heart-shaped green leaves.
- Application: The thousand-year-old custom of chewing betel and dyeing teeth black protects the digestive tract of naval warfare residents; Betel leaf water washes away poisoned arrow wounds.

> Editorial principles: all sections below clearly state **TRUE HISTORY** (artifacts, relics, bibliographies that can be verified) and **CONTROLLED INTERPRETATION** (filmic literature deduced from historical documents, serving to set the scene - not impersonating historical events).

### A. Standard chronological axis of the corpus (uniform use of all appendices)

- Liangzhu ~3300–2300 BC (Yangtze River basin, jade civilization): the starting point of Bach Viet Thien Co in the corpus.
- Dong Son ~1000 BC–100 AD (Red River Delta, Ma River, Ca River): Dong Son drum, three-sided bronze arrow, bronze jar.
- Co Loa ~257 BC (Dong Anh, Hanoi): 9-round snail wall, magic crossbow, Co Loa bronze arrows.
- Me Linh 40–43 AD (72 Me Linh caves according to the legend of Hai Ba Trung, the base of the uprising).
- Bach Dang 938 (Ngo Quyen) & Bach Dang 1288 (Tran Hung Dao - Hung Dao Vuong, Van Kiep, Yet Kieu chiseled the boat).

### B. Verify each group of herbs in the village

#### B1. Cinnamon Ngoc Tram (Thanh Hoa Cinnamon) — TRUE HISTORY

- Real place: Thuong Xuan district, Lang Chanh (Thanh Hoa), Pu Luong - Cuc Phuong limestone area; Ngoc Lac cinnamon market is still open today.
- Real bibliography: Hai Thuong Lan Ong Le Huu Trac, Hai Thuong Y Tong Tam Linh (18th century), treatise on cinnamon, father and son, and anise yang; Tue Tinh, Nam Duoc Than Hieu (14th century) recorded cinnamon as a warming medicine.
- Real artifacts: pieces of dried cinnamon bark in medicinal packages from the shipwreck of Cu Lao Cham (15th century, Hoi An Museum); Cinnamon bark in the medicinal herb cellar of Hoi An ancient town.
- True custom: Thai and Muong people use cinnamon bark to cook water to bathe after giving birth, soak cinnamon wine to massage the shoulders and neck when returning from the forest - still alive today.
- CONTROLLED INTERPRETATION: the detail of An Thai holding warm cinnamon wine under his tongue while crossing a cold mountain pass is a cinematic scene based on the old soldiers' custom of sucking ginger and drinking warm wine; Do not pretend to be the official method.

#### B2. Agarwood & Ky Nam (Truong Son) — TRUE HISTORICAL DATA

- Real places: Khanh Hoa (Van Ninh, Cam Ranh), Quang Nam (Tien Phuoc, Tra My), Phu Yen - the three largest agarwood granaries; Nha Trang agarwood street.
- Real bibliography: Le Quy Don, Phu Bien Tap Luc (1776) records tax on agarwood and agarwood of Thuan Quang; History of the Nguyen dynasty recorded offerings of agarwood; Periplus (1st century) and Arabic bibliographies of the 9th–10th centuries recorded Southern agarwood sold to West Asia.
- Real artifacts: Chu Dau ceramic incense burner with brown glaze (14th century, Hai Duong Museum); Hue royal lacquer agarwood box (Hue Museum of Antiquities); Agarwood ring in Quang Nam compound tomb.
- CONTROLLED IMAGINATION: Agarwood smoke rolling straight up into the open sky in the scene of worshiping Heaven is an image technique (real agarwood smoke scatters with the wind); intended to describe sincerity flying straight, not asserting physical properties.

#### B3. Ngai Nhung, Wind Ginger, Ink Grass, Horsetail, Xuyen Tam Lien — TRUE HISTORY

- Real bibliographies: Tue Tinh, Nam Duoc Than Hieu and Hong Nghia Giac Tu Y Thu (14th century) recorded that mugwort, ginger, plantain, and squid grass (Dragonberry grass) stopped bleeding, eliminated rheumatism, and were diuretic; Hai Thuong Y Tong Tam Linh systematized the magic of moxibustion.
- Real places: Luoc river bank (Hung Yen - Hai Duong), Hoang Su Phi terraced fields (Ha Giang), Van Kiep stream (Chi Linh, Hai Duong) - where legend has it the Tran army used forest leaves to heal wounds.
- Archeology: stone medicine mortar in Dong Son site (Thanh Hoa Museum); Bronze medicinal knife in Sino-Vietnamese tombs (National Museum of History).
- CONTROLLED INTERPRETATION: the scene of pounding squid grass and applying it to a cut wound is a reasonable reproduction from medical books (external poultice), simplifying the modern antiseptic process; When editing the film, it is necessary to note that this is an ancient practice and does not replace today's medical practice.

#### B4. Betel and Areca — TRUE HISTORY

- Real bibliography: Linh Nam Chich Quai (14th – 15th centuries) story Trau Cau (Tan - Lang); Complete History of Dai Viet records the Vietnamese custom of chewing betel and dyeing their teeth black.
- Real artifacts: Dong Son bronze betel jar, Chu Dau brown ceramic lime jar (Hai Duong Museum); 19th century lacquered betel tray (Museum of Ethnology).
- Real places: Cao Nhan areca village (Thuy Nguyen, Hai Phong), Vi Thuy betel garden (Hau Giang), Cho Lon betel market.
- CONTROLLED MYTH: betel leaf water to wash poisoned arrow stains is an inference from folk antiseptic properties; The design is kept at the level of field first aid, the character Moc Tinh Lan always adds warm ginger water - literary details, not sketches.

#### B5. Tay Ho Lotus, Moc Te, Don Do, Lac Tien, Mang Tang, Co Bang — TRUE HISTORY

- Real places: Tay Ho lotus pond (Hanoi), Quang Ba lotus tea marinating village; tea hill - osmanthus field in Duong Lam ancient village (Son Tay); Nga Son mat (Thanh Hoa) from almond grass and sedge grass.
- Real bibliography: Ly - Tran's poems with lotus, chrysanthemum, and moc te (Thien Uyen Tap Anh, poems of Tran Nhan Tong, Huyen Quang); Truc Lam's tea book (cited in the Dai Viet Su Ky Toan Thu Tran century version).
- Real artifacts: Chu Dau ceramic tea cup with brown lotus (Hai Duong Museum); Nga Son sedge mat in the warehouse of ethnological artifacts; 18th century copper lotus kettle.
- CONTROLLED PRACTICE: Marinating tea in the heart of a lotus flower overnight is a true technique of Quang Ba artisans; The detail of embalming Giao Linh silk clothes with osmanthus incense is an aesthetic deduction from the custom of embalming royal clothes - clearly stated as scene setting.
