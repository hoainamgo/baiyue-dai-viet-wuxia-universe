## 🏺 II. ARCHAEOLOGICAL EVIDENCE OF SMOKE Incense / Agarwood CULTURE

### 1. Evidence in Vietnam (Dong Son Culture, 5th Century BC - 1st Century AD):
* **Archaeological artifacts:** The **Bronze Incense Burners** were excavated in **Vac Village (Nghe An)**, **Viet Tri (Phu Tho)** and the Red River and Ma River basins.
* **Shaping & Art:** The body of the bronze censer is hollow, the lid has air holes carved with images of **flying Lac birds, star deer, water wave patterns and fishing buoys**.
* **Archaeobotany Analysis:** Charcoal residue taken from the bottom of Dong Son bronze urns contains traces of essential oil of tree resin **Aquilaria crassna - Dai Viet Agarwood**, vetiver roots, cinnamon and anise bark.
* **Function & Identity:** Ancient Vietnamese people burned herbal incense to:
  1. Repels damp air and mosquitoes in jungles and tropical rivers.
  2. Offering pure fragrance to Heaven and Earth during festivals to pray for a prosperous harvest.
  3. Heating the living space of a thatched-roof house on stilts.

### 2. Evidence from the Lower Yangtze Region (Liangzhu Culture, 3,300 BC – 2,300 BC, 5,300 years ago):
* Excavation of **Perforated Pottery Incense Burners** placed on open sacrificial mounds, next to *Ngoc Tong (Cong)* and *Ngoc Bich (Bi)* jade to offer thanksgiving to the Creator.

### 3. Origin of incense sticks (Bamboo toothpicks coated with incense powder):
* Compressing incense sticks in the form of bamboo sticks only appeared later around **II - VI centuries AD** through merchant ships traveling from India and the Persian Gulf region introducing the technique of binding herbal powder with Litsea glue.

## 📄 III. ARCHAEOLOGICAL & HISTORICAL EVIDENCE ABOUT THE PRACTICE OF BURNING VOCAL GOLD

Archeology confirms: **During the era of Hung Kings, An Duong Vuong and Dong Son Culture, the ancient Vietnamese people had ABSOLUTELY NO PRACTICE OF BURNING GOLD.**

### 1. Historical progress in replacing burial items:
* **Ancient times in the North:** Cruel customs **Burial (Burying real living people and animals according to kings)**.
* **Dynasties of Zhou - Warring States - Qin:** Replaced burial with **Ming Qi (Terracotta/wooden statue)** - typically the Terracotta Army of Qin Shi Huang.
* **A.D. 105 (Eastern Han Dynasty):** **Thai Lun (Cai Lun)** perfected the technique of making paper from bark and rags. Paper during this period was extremely expensive and was only used to record royal documents.

### 2. The birth of "votive paper money" (commercial fraud):
* **Historical records (*Han Shu*, *Thong Dien* of Du Huu in the Tang Dynasty):**
  * Thai Luan's nephew **Vuong Luan (Wang Lun)** due to business losses and left behind a large amount of waste paper, so he and his wife plotted to fake his death.
  * When neighbors came to pay their respects, Vuong Luan's wife brought files of scrap paper cut into the shape of coins to burn in front of the coffin. Suddenly Vuong Luan opened the coffin lid and came back to life and announced: *"Thanks to burning paper money to bribe the devil in the underworld, my life was returned to the earthly world!"*
  * People were confused and believed it, rushing to buy waste paper to burn and offer to the dead, thus forming the profession of making votive papers.
* **Formalized at court (AD 738 - Tang Xuanzong Dynasty):**
  * In order to prevent the practice of burying real copper coins, causing a shortage of national currency, the official of the Ministry of Rites **Wang Yu (Wang Yu)** submitted a petition asking the court for official permission to use paper money to replace copper coins in ceremonies.
  * This custom followed the Northern colonial mandarins who invaded Giao Chi during the Northern domination period.

### 3. Actual Archaeological Evidence:
* Pure Dong Son burial sites in Vietnam only found bronze pots, bronze axes, fossilized grains of rice, and agate beads — **absolutely no ashes of paper or votive papers**.
* The earliest perforated banknotes were only excavated at **Mogao Cave (Dunhuang, Gansu)** and the Xinjiang site dated to the **Sui – Tang period (6th – 8th centuries AD)**.
