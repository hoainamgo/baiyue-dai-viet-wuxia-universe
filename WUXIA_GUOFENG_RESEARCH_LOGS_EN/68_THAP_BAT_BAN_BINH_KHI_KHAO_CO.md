## 🗡️ PART I: GROUP 1 — CLOSE COMBAT COMBAT (CONTROLLING IN NARROWS & RIVERS)

### 1. Dong Son Bronze Sword (First Ancient Sword 2,500 Years Old):
- Archaeological evidence: Viet Khe Boat Tomb Site (Hai Phong), Elephant Mountain (Thanh Hoa).
- Appearance & Structure: Top-notch technical monolithic bronze casting. The sword's hilt is carved from a monolithic statue of a noble woman wearing a flared skirt, a warrior carrying each other, or a magical snake wrapped around it; short T-shaped armrest; The double-edged bamboo bud-shaped blade is slim and sharp.
- Techniques: Penetrating, evading close combat, not used for heavy cutting.

### 2. Dai Viet Long Sword of Ly - Tran Dynasties (Sword of Royalty and Knighthood):
- Archeology: Excavation of Thang Long Imperial Citadel, Bach Dang River.
- Appearance: The sword body is straight and elegant, tempered with mild steel and jade well water, and has extremely high elasticity. The sword hilt is made of ironwood or covered with stingray leather, carved with lotus flowers, leaves of the Ly dynasty or chrysanthemums of the Tran dynasty. Lacquered wooden scabbard inlaid with conch or covered with bronze.
- Technique: Pierce acupuncture points, push back as supple as apricot branch of Truc Lam Yen Tu (Truc Kiem Thien Tam).

### 3. Rice Leaf Short Knife (Lac Viet Dao Phap — Water War Knife):
- Archeology: Excavation of Luoc River relic site and Van Don trading port.
- Shape: The blade is 40-50cm long, the belly of the blade is slightly curved like a rice leaf, and the spine is thick and thick. The end of the knife's hilt is forged with an iron ring to thread a cloth strap to tighten around the wrist (to prevent the knife from falling when diving at the bottom of the river).
- Technique: Wriggling and slashing knee tendons, cutting the boat's anchor rope, combining 21 neck-snapping attacks to launch close combat.

### 4. Dong Son Dagger (T-shaped handle & Statue hilt):
- Archeology: Vac Village (Nghe An), Go Mon (Phu Tho).
- Appearance: 25-35cm long, the hilt is cast as a statue of a brave warrior wearing a loincloth or a wild animal. The sharp triangular blade has grooves that cut blood along the body.
- Technique: Unbreakable weapon tucked behind the waist, specialized for hand-to-hand combat, cutting the enemy general's throat when approaching.

### 5. Dong Son Heel Ax & Dong Son Spread Ax (Multi-Purpose War Ax):
- Archeology: Popular throughout the Red River and Ma River basins.
- Morphology: The ax head is curved in the shape of a shoe (ancient shoe) with a perpendicular wooden handle. The ax face is carved with images of flying Lac birds, star deer, and brave men rowing boats.
- Technique: While slashing and breaking the helmet, use the hooked nose to pull the shield or knock the opponent down.

### 6. Ivory Bamboo Cone & Bronze-Coated Tam Vong:
- Folk origin: Originated from the images of Thanh Giong bamboo stick and Bach Viet bamboo stick.
- Appearance: Old male bamboo (tam vong) soaked in pond mud for 3 years, exposed to wind and frost, tough enough to cut without breaking, both ends covered with grooved brass.
- Technique: Bat Quai Kun Phap, smashing the protective shield, sweeping down a series of enemy infantry.

## 🛡️ PART II: GROUP 2 — BATTLE SCHOOLS (DESTROYING THE WAR & EAST A BATTLE)

### 7. Go Liem Giao (Number 1 Carving of the Mongolian Cavalry):
- Archeology: Excavation of Van Kiep (Hai Duong) and Bach Dang bases.
- Appearance: The wooden handle is 2m - 2m4 long, the tip of the spear is sharp and combined with a sharp hooked blade bent backwards.
- Technique: Do not attack directly; The infantry slithered low, severing the tendons in the horse's legs, pulling off the armor belt or hooking the knight to the ground for the group of swordsmen to destroy.

### 8. Dong Son Sugarcane Bud Spear (Dong Nhon Spear):
- Archeology: Dong Son site, Ma river, Van Kiep.
- Appearance: The tip of the spear is made of copper/cold iron with a flattened cane leaf shape that expands in the middle, the spine of the spear has reinforced ribs, creating a diamond-shaped wound that is difficult to heal.
- Technique: Form a thick wall of bronze spears to block the advance of war elephants and cavalry.

### 9. Truong Dao Dong A (Infantry Slashing Knife):
- Archeology: Tran Temple relic site (Nam Dinh), Binh Than wharf.
- Appearance: The wooden handle is 1m6 - 1m8 long and covered with copper in many sections. The blade is large and heavy, focusing on the tip of the knife, without any flashy ornate bends.
- Technique: Horse-slaying sword technique, both hands put all their strength into cutting iron armor and heavy wooden shields.

### 10. Mark Long Bach Viet (Short Thuong Song Nuoc):
- Archeology: The Dong Son cultural layer is late to the Dinh - Early Le dynasties.
- Morphology: 40cm long flat blade inserted into a 1m5 long male bamboo handle, can both stab and slash across the side of the boat.

## 🎯 PART III: GROUP 3 — RANGED WEAPONS, SPIRITUAL CROSSBOWS & BACH VIET DARK ARROWS

### 11. Lien Chau Co Loa Divine Crossbow (Peak of Ancient Mechanical Engineering):
- Archaeological evidence: Tens of thousands of three-sided bronze arrows in the shape of swallow wings unearthed at Cau Vuc (Co Loa) and bronze crossbow bolts on display at the National History Museum.
- Mechanism: The bronze crossbow has a three-step trigger box and a brake shaft that holds multiple bowstrings at the same time. When pulling the trigger, the crossbow releases a series of three-pronged bronze arrows that penetrate leather armor and wooden shields at a distance of over 100 steps.

### 12. Bach Viet Ivory Bamboo Horn Bow:
- Morphology: The bow is assembled from old bamboo and water buffalo horns, glued with buffalo hide and wrapped with deer tendons soaked in wax. The short bow is mobile and can shoot flexibly on horseback and boat.

### 13. Bamboo Pipe Arrow Shooting To Eliminate Poison:
- Background of the sacred forest and poisonous water: Hollow bamboo tube 60-80cm long, use air to blow on it and impregnate it with Sui tree resin (Doc Tien Moc) or red-tailed viper venom. Hitting the target stops the prey's heart in the blink of an eye.

### 14. Swallow Wing Dart (Yen Vi Tieu):
- Appearance: Thin piece of cold steel, beveled at both ends, sharp and bent like a swallow's tail. When launched, it rotates in a wavy orbit, cutting through the wind without making a sound.

## 🌊 PART IV: GROUP 4 — MARINE WAR EQUIPMENT, PROTECTION & ARCHAEOLOGICAL ITEMS

### 15. Naval War Tool Set: Iron Drill & Boat Bottom Hammer:
- Representative character: Yet Kieu and the Lac Long navy.
- Archeology: Relics of Bach Dang river wharf, Van Don.
- Structure: Sharp spiral cold-forged steel drill combined with a heavy wooden hammer soaked in oil, with a reed tube in its mouth to pierce O Ham boat of O Ma Nhi.

### 16. Bach Dang Stake Beach (Strategic Wonder):
- Archeology: Yen Giang Stake Site, Van Muoi Field, Ma Ngua Field (Quang Ninh).
- Structure: The trunk of an ironwood tree is 20-30cm in diameter, sharpened at the tip, covered with heated iron, and placed at a 45-degree angle along the receding tide to pierce the enemy's boat.

### 17. Archaeological Armor (Guardian Plate & Chopstick Scale Buffalo Leather Armor):
- Archeology: Dong Son bronze breastplate with square/round shape cast with embossed sun pattern; The buffalo leather armor was made from fish scales of the Tran Dynasty and was flexible, soaked in salt water and did not rot.

### 18. Doc Moc Boat & Mong Dong Dong A Boat:
- Archeology: Viet Khe boat tomb (hollowed tree trunk coffin 4.76m long); The Mong Dong boat has a shallow bottom and a pointed nose covered in copper, gliding like flying through the mangrove beaches.
